# @jsayram Content Strategy — Research Notes

Channel: @jsayram, 542 subscribers (Jose's biggest channel). Open-ended personal channel:
current/tech topics, self-improvement, "start before perfect"-type content about the gap between
wanting to create and actually doing it. This doc is the standing reference for topic selection
on this channel going forward — read it during Step 1 (Research) of the `create-video` skill
whenever the channel is `@jsayram`.

Reference video already made: `videos/start-before-perfect` — faceless narrated ~3-minute video,
disposable-camera film-photo visual style, friend-to-friend second-person voice, no cast, no
call-to-action ending, 32 lines / ~176s.

---

## Niche positioning

The proof that this format works isn't hypothetical — it's the dominant pattern in the
self-improvement faceless-essay space right now. Two channels sit at the top of that exact
lane and validate the approach at scale:

- **Einzelgänger** — stoicism/philosophy essays, past 1.5M subscribers, high retention on
  15-minute videos, no host on camera, quiet narration over symbolic/atmospheric visuals.
- **Improvement Pill** — 3.77M subscribers, one core promise repeated video after video ("an idea
  that can change how you live"), same faceless-narration-over-visuals structure.

Neither of these creators appears on camera and neither leans on production polish. The idea and
the voice carry the video — which is exactly what `start-before-perfect` already does for this
channel. The format is proven; the question is which specific ideas earn a slot in it.

At 542 subscribers, @jsayram is past the "does this format work at all" question and into "which
recurring angle does this specific channel own." Broad "self-improvement" is saturated at the
mega-channel level (Improvement Pill, Einzelgänger, the whole "motivational voiceover" ecosystem).
What isn't saturated: the *very specific, unglamorous, confessional* angle `start-before-perfect`
already nailed — not "5 tips to stop procrastinating" but "here is the exact humiliating
internal monologue you have at 2am, and here's the one that isn't true." That specificity, said
in second person, friend-to-friend, is the differentiator worth committing to rather than
drifting into generic motivational-quote territory.

Research also confirms the current platform mood favors this: 2026 trend coverage repeatedly
flags a swing toward "authenticity over production" — simpler thumbnails, less-polished edits,
slower pacing, intimacy over spectacle — which is the same lane `start-before-perfect` is already
in, not a pivot required.

## Content pillars

Four recurring pillars, each a variation on the same emotional mechanism `start-before-perfect`
used: name the specific self-deceiving thought, expose it, then hand back a smaller, truer
standard than the one the viewer is holding themselves to. Rotate across these four rather than
repeating one:

1. **Creative paralysis / the gap between planning and doing.** Direct continuation of
   `start-before-perfect`. Research, gear, "just one more tutorial," the notes app graveyard —
   anything where the stalling *looks* like productivity. This is the channel's proven pillar;
   it should recur most often.

2. **Comparison and the invisible scoreboard.** Comparing your rough draft to someone else's
   finished, filtered output — not just on social media broadly, but specifically the creator/
   side-project version of this (your unfinished channel vs. their 200th upload, your idea vs.
   their shipped product). Directly validated by real search demand: "comparison trap" content
   is an active, recurring 2026 topic across mental-health and creator-economy coverage, and it's
   a one-beat extension of the exact reframe already used in scene 20 of `start-before-perfect`
   ("comparing your first attempt to their two-hundredth video was never a fair fight").

3. **Burnout and the always-on self.** The exhaustion of treating rest as a productivity failure,
   creator/side-hustle burnout specifically (unstable output, constant comparison, weak
   boundaries) rather than corporate-9-to-5 burnout — keeps it aimed at the same
   creating-something-of-your-own audience as pillar 1, rather than drifting into generic
   workplace-wellness content that's a different (and more crowded) niche.

4. **Tech-adjacent anxiety, aimed at the individual, not the industry.** Not "will AI take my
   job" think-pieces (that's a saturated, fast-moving news-cycle lane this channel shouldn't
   compete in) — instead the personal version: feeling behind on tools you "should" already know,
   the fear that everyone else is moving faster, using AI as a stalling tactic instead of the
   camera/mic-buying stall in `start-before-perfect`. Research shows this personal-anxiety angle
   (as opposed to industry-analysis) is under-served relative to demand — most AI content is
   either hype or doom, very little of it is "here's the specific quiet dread you feel."

Pillars 1 and 2 should anchor the near-term slate (they're closest to proven and to each other in
tone); 3 and 4 are the next ring out once the first two have a few videos each.

## Validated video ideas

Ten ideas, each checked against real demand rather than invented from scratch — either a proven
video/format in this space, a named real phenomenon with documented specifics, or a direct
extension of a beat already validated in `start-before-perfect`. All in the same second-person,
no-cast, no-CTA format.

1. **"Your Notes App Is a Graveyard"** (Pillar 1). Built on a real documented case: a creator who
   catalogued 879 open browser tabs, 1,501 screenshots, 4,026+ "watch later" videos, 307 notes
   app entries — every one of them an idea that never became anything. Concrete, countable,
   visual (stacks of index cards / notebooks already exist as a motif from `start-before-perfect`
   scene 25). Strong hook: viewers can go check their own notes app mid-video.

2. **"The Version of You Comparing Isn't Real"** (Pillar 2). Direct sequel to
   `start-before-perfect` scene 20's "not a fair fight" beat, expanded to its own video: you're
   not comparing yourself to a person, you're comparing yourself to their highlight reel, which
   is validated as an active, recurring 2026 topic in mental-health and creator-economy coverage.

3. **"You Don't Need a Following First"** (Pillar 1/2 crossover). Expands scene 11 of
   `start-before-perfect` ("every following started with zero people watching") into its own
   video — the backwards logic of waiting for permission from an audience that doesn't exist yet.

4. **"Burnout Isn't Laziness, It's a Debt You Didn't Notice Accruing"** (Pillar 3). Reframes
   burnout away from the corporate-wellness framing (rest as HR policy) toward the creator/
   side-project framing: burnout as the bill coming due for months of low-grade always-on
   pressure you told yourself wasn't a big deal.

5. **"You're Not Behind on AI, You're Just Scared to Look Dumb Using It"** (Pillar 4). Mirrors the
   exact gear-excuse structure from `start-before-perfect` scenes 8–10 (buying equipment instead
   of using it) but aimed at tools instead of cameras — the new version of "I need better gear
   first" is "I need to fully understand this AI tool first."

6. **"The Standard You're Failing Doesn't Exist"** (Pillar 2). Standalone expansion of scene 22 —
   the invisible, self-invented bar nobody else is holding you to, generalized beyond just
   "posting your first video" to any unstarted thing (business, workout, book, apology).

7. **"Research Is the Most Respectable Way to Stall"** (Pillar 1). Direct sequel to scene 6 —
   a whole video on "productivity theater," a real named, currently-discussed phenomenon:
   activity that looks like progress and produces the feeling of progress without moving anything
   forward. Tutorials, courses, threads, templates — all consumption dressed as preparation.

8. **"Nobody Remembers Your First Anything"** (Pillar 2). Extension of scene 17's "one old video,
   zero views" beat — applied broadly: first attempts at anything are, provably, forgotten faster
   than the person making them assumes; the embarrassment they're avoiding barely registers to
   anyone else.

9. **"The 2am Thought That Isn't True"** (Pillar 3, tone-setter/anchor video). A direct-address
   video built around the specific late-night self-talk loop — the exhausted, wired, "everyone
   else has it figured out" spiral — named and then dismantled the way `start-before-perfect`
   dismantled the gear excuse. Anchors the burnout pillar the way the original video anchors
   pillar 1.

10. **"You Already Have Enough to Start"** (Pillar 1/4 crossover, closing-the-loop video). A
    direct answer video for once several of the above exist — collects the recurring "you're
    waiting for a permission that isn't coming" thread across gear, following, readiness, and
    tools into one video, positioned as a loose spiritual sequel/companion to
    `start-before-perfect` rather than a rehash of it.

## Competitive landscape

- **Einzelgänger** (1.5M+ subs) — stoicism/philosophy essays, 15-minute format, faceless
  narration over symbolic visuals. Proves long-form faceless philosophical content has durable
  demand at scale; too long and too philosophy-branded to compete with directly, but confirms the
  format.
- **Improvement Pill** (3.77M subs) — single-idea motivational essays, faceless. Proves the core
  mechanic (one idea, one reframe, narrated) scales huge; also shows the ceiling of *generic*
  motivational framing — growth has reportedly slowed, which argues for @jsayram's more specific,
  confessional angle over competing on the same broad "life advice" ground.
- **Burnout-focused smaller channels** (e.g., "Burnout Explained" and similar) — confirm burnout
  content has an audience distinct from general self-improvement, but tends to be presented in a
  clinical/explainer register rather than the friend-to-friend voice this channel uses — an
  opening for the same topic told differently.
- **Broader creator-economy signal**: 2026 coverage consistently notes audiences moving toward
  "authenticity over production" — simpler thumbnails, less-polished edits, more intimate pacing
  — and toward video podcasts / long-form conversation. That favors staying with the plain,
  unhurried `start-before-perfect` register rather than adding polish, spectacle, or a faster cut
  rate to compete with bigger channels.
- **Gap in the market**: most AI-anxiety content is written for industry-watchers (will AI take
  jobs, what's the next model) rather than for the individual side-project/creator audience
  feeling personally behind. Pillar 4 sits in that gap.

## Visual style recommendation

**Confirmed: keep `disposable-camera`.** Nothing in this research argues for changing it.

Reasoning:

- The pillars above (paralysis, comparison, burnout, tech-stalling) are all extensions of the
  same intimate, confessional, slightly-worn emotional register as `start-before-perfect` — the
  grainy, warm, personal-photograph look is doing real work matching that tone, not just a style
  default.
- The 2026 trend research independently points the same direction: audiences are moving toward
  less-polished, more intimate visual presentation, which is what `disposable-camera` already is
  relative to the clean-vector or glossy-photographic alternatives in this pipeline.
- The object-metaphor visual language already built for this channel (closed laptops, unopened
  boxes, empty chairs, notebooks, phones glowing in the dark) maps directly onto every new idea
  above without needing new visual vocabulary — index cards for the notes-app video, an unopened
  book/course for the AI-stalling video, a clock/empty mug for the burnout video.

One deliberate variation worth considering, not a replacement: for pillar 4 (tech-adjacent
anxiety) specifically, `nostalgic-vhs-2000` is already in this pipeline's style library and could
be used as an occasional one-off variant when a video's subject is explicitly about technology
moving faster than you — the slightly-degraded video-tape texture reads as "outdated tech" in a
way disposable-camera doesn't. This is a maybe, not a recommendation to switch defaults; the
channel's identity should stay anchored to `disposable-camera` as the default across pillars 1–3,
with pillar 4 as the only place to test a variant.

**Update: tested and rejected.** `nostalgic-vhs-2000` was actually test-rendered (with the
required `v8s` trigger word) and does not produce a VHS/tape look at all under this pipeline's
turbo settings — it renders as a painted/illustrated distressed style instead, with a recurring
cracked-screen motif unrelated to the intended effect. See `videos/test-vhs-journal/` and
`SKILL.md`'s channel-profile notes. Stay on `disposable-camera` for all pillars until this
style gets tuned and re-validated at different settings.
